# What actually moves a credit score in India

CIBIL (and the other three bureaus — Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark)
scores range 300–900. This is general education, not a personalized
credit plan — FinBuddy doesn't pull a user's actual bureau report.

**Roughly, in order of weight:**

1. **Payment history (~35%).** The single biggest factor. Even one missed
   EMI or credit card payment, reported 30+ days late, can meaningfully
   drop a score and stays on the report for years.
2. **Credit utilization (~30%).** The percentage of total available credit
   card limit actually being used, at statement time. Keeping utilization
   under ~30% is the common guideline; maxing out a card every month, even
   if paid in full afterward, can hurt the score because bureaus often see
   the statement-date snapshot, not the post-payment balance.
3. **Length of credit history (~15%).** Older accounts help. Closing the
   oldest credit card on a whim shortens average account age.
4. **Credit mix (~10%).** A mix of secured (e.g. a car/home loan) and
   unsecured (credit cards, personal loans) credit is viewed slightly
   favorably over having only one type — a minor factor, not worth taking
   on a loan just to "diversify."
5. **New credit / hard inquiries (~10%).** Applying for several loans or
   cards in a short window generates multiple hard inquiries and can
   suggest credit-seeking stress to a lender, even if each individual
   application is reasonable.

**A frequently-confused point relevant to the EMI-decode use case:**
converting a purchase to EMI on an *existing* credit card is generally
treated as using existing credit, not a new hard inquiry — but it does
raise utilization if the EMI'd amount stays counted against the card's
limit rather than being carved out, depending on the issuer. Worth noting
as "varies by issuer" rather than a firm universal rule.
